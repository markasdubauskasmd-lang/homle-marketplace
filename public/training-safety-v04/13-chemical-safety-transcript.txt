COSHH and PPE: safer products, safer work
Draft v0.4; AI narration; not accredited certification.

Start with the task
Welcome to Homlle chemical safety awareness. Before using a product, check that it is approved for the task and that you understand the instructions and task assessment. This short lesson does not qualify you to handle every chemical. If you are unsure, stop and ask a competent person.

What COSHH means
COSHH means Control of Substances Hazardous to Health. In Great Britain, the 2002 regulations require risks from harmful substances to be controlled. Northern Ireland has separate 2003 regulations. These lessons support awareness; they are not an accredited qualification. Homlle must arrange competent, task-specific review before using them to authorise work.

How exposure happens
A hazard is something that can harm you. Risk depends on how you use it and who is exposed. Think about breathing in sprays, splashes onto skin or eyes, and contaminated hands near food. A beauty product or an everyday detergent can still cause harm. Consider the customer and anyone nearby too.

The sheet is not the assessment
The safety data sheet helps explain a product, but it is not the assessment of your job. Before work, find the approved method for the product, surface, room and people present. For example, a small bathroom may need a different method from an open work area. If the controls cannot be followed, pause and ask.

Control exposure first
First ask whether the hazardous substance or process can be avoided. If not, use a safer approved alternative and controls that reduce exposure, such as an appropriate dispensing method or effective extraction. Protective equipment is an extra layer, not permission to work with uncontrolled exposure. Never substitute products on your own.

Keep the label
Check the product name and label before each use. Keep the label readable and follow the manufacturer instructions. Never put a chemical into a food or drink container. An unlabelled bottle is not safe to guess with. Set it aside safely and ask for an approved replacement.

Never mix products
Do not mix cleaning products, even if both seem familiar. Mixing can create dangerous reactions. More concentrated does not mean safer or better. Follow the stated dilution and method for the product and surface. If a customer asks for a different mixture, explain that you must follow the safe approved method.

Protect people and the space
Before starting, check ventilation, storage and the protective equipment specified for this task. Keep products away from children and pets. Gloves must be suitable for the product and in good condition. If you notice skin irritation, breathing discomfort or another concern, stop exposure and report it through the agreed support route.

PPE must match the task
PPE means personal protective equipment. The assessment decides whether chemical-resistant gloves, eye protection, protective clothing or other equipment is needed. Check the items fit you and work together. A glove material that suits one product may fail with another. Use the specified material and replacement instructions, not a guess based on colour.

Use protection properly
Check gloves for damage before use. Follow the trained removal method so the contaminated outside does not touch bare skin. Dispose of single-use gloves correctly; clean reusable equipment only as instructed. Wash and dry your hands afterwards. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene. Report red, itchy or cracked skin early and ask for a review.

A mask is not a shortcut
A basic face covering is not protection against chemical vapour. Where a risk assessment requires respiratory protection, a competent person must select equipment suitable for the substance and wearer. Tight-fitting respirators need a face fit test and a fit check each time they are worn. Do not start a respirator-dependent task without the required training and equipment.

Beauty work needs its own controls
For beauty work, assess the actual treatment. Nail dust, solvents and repeated wet work need suitable controls; hair products can also cause skin or breathing problems. Use product-specific instructions and any required extraction. This lesson does not qualify you to carry out a treatment or replace consultation, allergy precautions, infection control or practical beauty training.

Store, close and separate
Keep lids closed and products secured so they cannot spill or be accessed by children or pets. Separate incompatible products according to their instructions. Do not decant into food or drink containers. Use the agreed disposal route for residues and contaminated materials. Do not pour an unfamiliar chemical down a drain. Ask if the route is unclear.

When something goes wrong
For a spill or exposure, stop work and keep other people away. Follow the product emergency instructions and get appropriate help. Do not try to neutralise an unknown chemical or enter unsafe air to rescue equipment. In an immediate emergency, contact emergency services. Report the product and what happened.

Scenario: the mystery bottle
A customer hands you an unlabelled bottle and says it works well. What should you do? Do not smell it to identify it, mix it or try it on a small patch. Explain that you need an approved labelled product. If none is available, pause the task and contact support.
