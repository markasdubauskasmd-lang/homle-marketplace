Manual handling: plan before you move
Draft v0.4; AI narration; not accredited certification.

Plan before moving
Welcome to Homlle manual handling awareness. Moving equipment or supplies can cause injury when the task is not planned. First consider whether hazardous handling can be avoided. If it cannot, assess the task and reduce the risk before starting. This lesson is not a practical lifting qualification.

The legal approach
The Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992 set the Great Britain approach: avoid hazardous handling where reasonably practicable, assess what remains and reduce injury risk. This includes carrying, pushing and pulling, not only lifting. Training alone cannot make an unsafe task safe. Northern Ireland has its own equivalent framework, which must be checked for work there.

Four questions before moving
Use four prompts: task, individual, load and environment. How often will you move it? Can you do so comfortably today? Is it awkward, unstable or hard to grip? Is the route clear and well lit? Report pain, fatigue or restrictions privately so the work can be adjusted. There is no single legal safe weight for every person and task.

Check the whole task
Look at the load, grip, route, stairs and available space. Think about how often you must move it and your own capability today. There is no single weight that makes every lift safe. A light awkward item on a wet staircase may still be unsafe. Pause if the conditions are unsuitable.

Make the task easier
Can you split supplies into smaller loads, use suitable equipment or arrange help? Clear the route before moving anything and check that the destination is ready. Help is useful only when the task is planned together. Do not use a customer as an unplanned lifting assistant for an unsafe task.

Use the assessed method
Follow the handling method you have been shown for the actual task and equipment. Keep movements controlled and avoid sudden twisting or jerking. If the load shifts, the route becomes unsafe or you feel pain, stop safely and reassess. Do not push through discomfort to finish a booking faster.

Moving a manageable load
For a load already assessed as manageable, plan where it will go and take a stable position. Get a secure grip and keep it close. Use a comfortable posture with some bending at the hips and knees, and move smoothly without jerking. Turn by moving your feet. Put it down before making a precise adjustment. Practise with a competent assessor.

Trolleys still need assessment
A suitable trolley may reduce carrying, but pushing and pulling still need assessment. Check its condition, capacity, route and your view ahead. A heavy beauty case can become hard to control on a slope. Do not take ordinary wheeled equipment down stairs. Agree a suitable method or change the task before moving it.

Working together safely
A second person does not automatically make a difficult load safe. Team handling must be planned for the load, route and both people. Agree who gives the signal and where to put it down. Either person can stop the move. Do not ask an unprepared customer to take part in a risky lift.

Footwear, repetition and symptoms
Use suitable footwear and any grip protection identified by the assessment. PPE does not reduce the weight of a load. Repeated reaching, scrubbing and carrying can build up strain even with small items. Adjust equipment and work organisation, and report discomfort early. Pause an unsafe task instead of pushing through pain.

Keep service boundaries
A customer may ask you to move a heavy sofa or help lift a person. These are not automatic additions to a cleaning or beauty booking. Do not attempt unassessed handling outside your competence and approved scope. Explain the boundary and contact support. Protecting safety must not cost you a positive rating.

Scenario: the loaded case
Your equipment case is so full that it blocks your view on narrow stairs. Should you carry it carefully anyway? No. Stop and change the plan. Split the load if safe, clear the route or arrange another assessed method. Taking a little longer is better than accepting a foreseeable injury risk.

Check and demonstrate
Complete the knowledge check, then demonstrate a safe representative task to a competent assessor. Explain when you would stop and how you would report a hazard or near miss. Record the assessment against this lesson version. A completed video does not prove competence for every load, person or piece of equipment. Your assessor should check understanding, correct any gaps and observe the relevant practical skills. Do not treat video completion as automatic permission to work.
