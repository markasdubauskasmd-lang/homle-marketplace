Safe visits: lone working and everyday safety
Draft v0.4; AI narration; not accredited certification.

Know your role
Welcome to Homlle safe visits awareness. Your role is to provide the agreed cleaning or beauty service. You are not a carer, babysitter or investigator. Before working alone, know the named duty contact, check-in plan and emergency route. These arrangements must actually be staffed and tested.

Plan a safe visit
Before the visit, check the address, access and known risks. Have a charged phone and an agreed check-in and check-out plan with someone who can respond. An app notification is not an emergency service. If the situation changes or you feel threatened, leave when safe and contact help.

A real check-in plan
Before a lone visit, agree who will respond, when contact is expected and what happens if you miss it. A phone notification that nobody monitors is not a safety system. Test the contact route without calling emergency services. If the phone fails or the risk is beyond the agreed limits, move to safety and pause the visit.

Leave an unsafe situation
If you encounter threats, aggression or conditions outside the safe plan, do not argue or try to prove you can cope. Leave safely where possible and seek help. Serious immediate danger needs emergency services. Tell the duty contact once safe. Homlle must set clear limits on what lone workers can do and arrange suitable support.

Protect the working area
Cleaning can create hazards for other people. Keep people away from wet slippery floors using suitable barriers or working in sections; a warning sign alone does not prevent access. Keep the exit clear and manage trailing cables. Only reopen the area when safe. Ask for help if you cannot separate the work from customers, children or pets.

Notice a concern
You may notice a person being threatened, neglected or pressured for money. Listen calmly and record relevant facts, keeping observation separate from opinion. Do not question people like an investigator, confront a suspected abuser or promise secrecy. Explain that you may need to share information with people who can help.

Urgent danger comes first
If someone is in immediate danger or an emergency crime is happening, call nine nine nine and move to safety if possible. Do not wait for a routine manager reply. Do not attempt an unsafe rescue. Once safe, report through the agreed Homlle route so further protective action can be coordinated.

Report securely
For a non-immediate concern, contact the safeguarding lead or deputy promptly and follow the appropriate local authority or police route when needed. Record times, observations, relevant words and actions. Use a secure channel and share only what is necessary. Do not post the situation in a group chat or public review.

Scenario: a request for secrecy
A customer says someone is threatening them and asks you to tell nobody. Do not promise secrecy. Listen, explain that help may require sharing relevant information, and assess immediate danger. Follow the safeguarding route and record facts securely. Your job is to raise the concern, not decide the whole case yourself.

Practise the response
Complete the knowledge check and rehearse how to contact help and respond to a missed check-in. A supervisor must confirm that the contact details and escalation plan work. Record the lesson and assessment. This awareness lesson does not replace a safeguarding lead, local procedures or a trained emergency response. Your assessor should check understanding, correct any gaps and observe the relevant practical skills. Do not treat video completion as automatic permission to work.
