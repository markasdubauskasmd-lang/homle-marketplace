What this costs

The customer's breakdown

    Core

    Room base prices

    The base price is what the room costs before extra tasks, and it includes the first few. Minutes are what the cleaner is committing to — keep them honest, because the cleaner's hourly floor is checked against them.

    Specialist tasks

    Priced individually rather than at the per-task rate. These are standalone jobs — an oven at the ordinary task price would pay a cleaner about a pound for forty minutes.

    Add-ons

    Service types and discounts

    Property condition

    What the scanner's read of a property does to its price. Level 5 — specialist review — is not listed and cannot be given a multiplier: it means a person needs to look before a cleaner is sent, and that is not a price.

    Where the property is

    Homle lists every UK postcode area, and one national rate set at the London band is 47% above market in most of the country. A postcode nobody has classified falls to the standard band, so an unrecognised address is never overcharged.

    Short notice and awkward hours

    Both reach the cleaner through the ordinary share, which is what makes a hard-to-fill slot worth accepting rather than only more expensive to buy.

    Promotion codes Never shown to customers

    A discount comes out of Homle's margin, never the Cleaner's pay — a Cleaner is paid on what the booking would have cost. A code deep enough that Homle cannot afford it is refused at quote time rather than sold.

    The code list is never served to a browser. A customer types a code and the server checks it; a code that does not exist and one that does not apply get the same answer, so the list cannot be guessed at.

    Cancellation

    Charged as a share of the booking with a cash cap, so cancelling a large clean does not produce a fee nobody will pay. What the cleaner receives out of it is below, with the rest of the commercial figures.

    Economics Never shown to customers

    The cleaner's share is the promise to supply. The floors below are what stops a booking being sold at a loss — a quote that breaks one is refused rather than discounted.
