For cleaners

You set the price. You see the job first.

Most cleaning platforms hand you an address, a time and a guess. Homle asks the customer to walk their home with a phone and confirm a room-by-room checklist before anyone is invited — so what you are quoting for is written down before you say yes.

Be straight about where this stands

Homle is new and is not pretending otherwise. Applying now puts you in the first group of cleaners on it. It also means we cannot promise you work — there is no queue of jobs waiting, and anybody who tells you there is on a platform this young is guessing. If you want a full book of clients from day one, this is not that yet.

What you control

  • Your prices. You set your own rate for each service you offer, hourly or a fixed price. What a customer is quoted is built from your rate, not handed down to you.
  • Your areas. You choose the postcode areas you will travel to. Work outside them is not offered to you.
  • Your hours. You add the windows you are actually available. A job that does not fit inside one of them never reaches you.
  • The kind of work. Homes, workplaces, or both — your choice, and it is applied when jobs are matched.
  • Each job. An invitation is an offer with a deadline, not an assignment. Declining costs you nothing.

What you see before you accept

  • The room-by-room checklist the customer reviewed and confirmed, including anything they explicitly excluded.
  • The exact amount you will be paid for the job, fixed at the point the booking is made and not adjustable afterwards.
  • The date, the time window and how long the work is scoped to take.
  • The area. The full address and access notes are released once the booking is confirmed, not before.

How you get paid

The customer's card is authorised before the job, so the money is confirmed as available rather than promised. After the clean is finished and the customer confirms it, the payment is taken and your share is transferred to the bank account on your profile.

Two things worth saying plainly. Payment goes through the platform, so there is no chasing an invoice — and equally, you are not employed by Homle. You are self-employed, you handle your own tax, and you price your own work.

What you will need to provide

Before a profile can go live it has to be complete and reviewed. You will be asked for:

  • Your personal and business details, and the bank account for payments.
  • Proof of identity, and proof of your right to work in the UK.
  • A DBS check.
  • Your cleaning experience.
  • Your own public liability insurance. Homle does not insure you. You provide the cover and the evidence of it.
  • What equipment and materials you bring.
  • The postcode areas you cover.

Your documents are encrypted and are read only when somebody at Homle is reviewing your application. Every time one of those reviews is opened, it is recorded against the person who opened it.

How the work runs

  1. Create your Cleaner account and complete your profile.
  2. Your identity and background checks are reviewed. Your profile becomes visible once it is complete and approved.
  3. Add the hours you are available and the areas you cover.
  4. Invitations arrive for jobs that fit. Open the checklist, see the pay, accept or decline.
  5. On the day, work through the checklist in the app, add before-and-after photos, and mark it finished.
  6. The customer confirms the work and payment is released.

Questions we get asked

Does it cost anything to join? No. There is no subscription and no joining fee. Homle earns from the difference between what the customer pays and what you are paid, which is set when the job is priced and shown to you before you accept.

Can I work somewhere else as well? Yes. Nothing here is exclusive. Set your available hours to whatever is genuinely free.

What if a customer asks for more on the day? Extra work is added to the job in the app and has to be agreed on price before it counts. Do not agree to unrecorded work — if it is not in the app, the payment does not cover it.

What if I need to cancel? Tell the customer through the booking as early as you can. Repeatedly cancelling accepted work is the sort of thing that will affect whether you keep getting invitations.
